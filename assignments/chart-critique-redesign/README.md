## The task

Use the supplied data to design an original figure that answers the instructor's question.
Create the figure in Python. Choose its visual form yourself; no starter chart is supplied
for redesign. The question may require you to compare several aspects of the data, but the
figure should communicate a clear answer to the intended reader.

## What to submit

**Submit only the figure with its accompanying notes.** Do not submit a notebook, script,
separate rationale, report, or other supporting files.

The figure and notes must stand on their own. Use readable labels, units and an informative
title. In the notes, explain the data source, sample, period, aggregation, filters and any
limitations needed to interpret the figure. Explain uncertainty if you show it. Identify
synthetic data as synthetic. Keep the notes concise and attached to the figure.

Include a brief disclosure of material AI assistance and how you checked it within the notes,
or state that there was none. No separate disclosure document is required.

## Before submitting

- Check that the figure answers the supplied question without overstating the evidence.
- Verify calculations, filters, units, missing-value handling and plotted values against the data.
- Inspect the exported figure and notes at normal reading size.
- Check that colour, contrast, scales and category order support accurate interpretation.
- Keep your working code for your own verification; it is not part of the submission.

This is individual work. AI tools are permitted under the course's **use, then verify** policy.
You remain responsible for the analysis and every visual choice.

## Rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Full-credit evidence in the figure and notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Answer to the question | 20 | Directly addresses the supplied question and communicates a supported answer for the intended reader. |
| Analytic correctness | 20 | Uses the correct observations, aggregation, comparisons and units; represents values accurately and avoids unsupported claims. |
| Encoding and design decisions | 25 | Uses an appropriate chart form, honest scales, useful ordering, colour and annotation; retains necessary context. |
| Communication and accessibility | 15 | Is legible at normal size, with clear labels and title, sufficient contrast and no unnecessary reliance on colour alone. |
| Figure notes and transparency | 20 | Provides concise, accurate context, provenance and limitations needed to interpret the figure, including uncertainty and AI assistance where relevant. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Code quality and a separate written rationale are not assessed. Visual polish cannot compensate
for an incorrect calculation or misleading presentation.
