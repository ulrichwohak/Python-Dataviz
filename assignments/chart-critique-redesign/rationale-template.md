## Audience, decision, and question

Who is the intended reader? What decision or discussion should the chart support? State the
specific question your redesign answers.

## Critique of the original

Explain briefly what the baseline chart gets right. Then identify its most consequential design
problems. Refer to evidence and course concepts such as visual encoding, hierarchy, comparison,
scale, annotation, cognitive load, and accessibility. Avoid describing a preference as if it were
a principle.

## Redesign decisions and trade-offs

Explain why you chose this chart form and how you used position, order, colour, labels, and
annotation. Name at least one reasonable alternative you rejected and the trade-off involved.

## Verification and limitations

Record the direct calculations you used to check at least two displayed values. Describe how you
checked filters, dates, units, missing values, and visual accessibility. State what the chart does
not establish or what context the reader still needs.

## AI-use disclosure

Name any AI tool that materially helped, what you used it for, and how you checked its output.
If there was no material AI assistance, write: “No material AI assistance used.”
