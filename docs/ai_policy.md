# AI Policy

AI coding assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, and similar) are **permitted** in this course
and are treated as a normal part of a modern analyst's workflow. The course position is:

> **Use AI, then verify.** You are responsible for understanding, checking, and standing
> behind anything an assistant produces on your behalf.

## What this means in practice

- **Understand before you submit.** If you cannot explain what a chart shows or why a line of
  code works, it is not ready to hand in.
- **Verify every AI-generated chart and claim.** Assistants can produce plausible but
  misleading visualisations, incorrect transformations, and interpretations unsupported by the
  data. Apply the verification checklist below to your work.
- **Cite assistance.** When AI materially shaped your project work, note it briefly (which
  tool, for what). This mirrors how professionals disclose tooling.
- **Protect data.** Class examples use public real data. Public availability does not remove privacy obligations. Do not paste personal, confidential, or
  otherwise restricted project data into external AI tools.

For each of the three individual figures (25% combined), put a brief AI-use disclosure and
verification statement in the figure notes; do not submit code or a separate disclosure file.
The final project's existing disclosure requirements remain unchanged.

## Verification checklist for AI-generated charts and code

1. Does the chart answer the actual question asked?
2. Are the axes, units, and scales correct and honest (no accidental truncation or dual axes)?
3. Is the chart type appropriate for the data (e.g. not a pie chart for many categories)?
4. Does the code run top-to-bottom on the actual course or project dataset, not a hallucinated
   substitute?
5. Are column names, aggregations, and filters what you intended?
6. Did it silently drop, duplicate, or mis-join rows?
7. Could a reader be misled by colour, ordering, or missing context?

If you cannot answer all seven, fix it before relying on the output.

## On repository creation

The initial repository structure and portions of the course material were drafted with AI
assistance and then reviewed and revised by the instructor. That disclosure is about repository
construction, not about how students should use AI in their coursework.
