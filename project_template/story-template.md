Use this brief to choose the amount of detail, terminology, visual emphasis, and delivery
format. Preserve the uncertainty and qualifications this reader needs to judge the evidence.

Before including each figure, ask:

- What should this reader be able to understand, compare, check, or investigate?
- What finding does the evidence support, or what question does the figure leave open?
- What would the reader lose if we removed it? Would a sentence or table do the job better?

Let the evidence determine the takeaway. A figure should help readers judge the argument,
including its limitations. An open question can contribute to the story without implying an answer.

## Opening

Give the reader only the context needed to understand why the question matters. Do not begin with
a catalogue of variables or methods.

Where useful, let the first evidence figure introduce the pattern or puzzle that motivates
the question. Separate what we observe, what might explain it, and what remains to be checked.
Say whether the pattern suggested the hypothesis or illustrates an idea you already held.
Identify summaries such as averages or fitted lines rather than calling them raw observations.
This can be the first chart below; an additional chart is not required.
